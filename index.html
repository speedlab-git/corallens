In one sentence

CoralLens watches only the frames that matter, lets a coral-tuned vision-language model reason across viewpoints, and writes monitoring reports in which every finding cites the frames that support it.

01 / Background

Reefs are filmed for hours. Reviewing that footage is the bottleneck.

Coral reefs face warming, bleaching, and disease, and underwater cameras now record far more footage than experts can review. Vision-language models (VLMs) can describe what they see, but reliable coral monitoring needs more than captions for single images: it needs efficient video analysis, consistent interpretation across viewpoints, and reports backed by visual evidence.

Obstacle 1 · Efficiency

Too many redundant frames

Survey videos hold thousands of near-duplicate frames. Processing all of them is slow; sampling at fixed intervals misses small colonies, algae, and early bleaching.

Obstacle 2 · Consistency

One colony, many appearances

Camera motion, lighting, turbidity, and occlusion change how a colony looks. Single-image VLMs give inconsistent answers and may count the same colony twice.

Obstacle 3 · Trust

Claims without evidence

Generated descriptions can overlook details or state conclusions the footage does not support, which scientists cannot accept in a monitoring report.

Can underwater video be turned efficiently into accurate, interpretable coral observations and monitoring reports in which every statement is supported by the recorded footage?

03 / Objectives

Three interconnected objectives.

Guided by CoralVQA's 16 coral-understanding dimensions (category, colour, shape, size, quantity, spatial distribution, structural integrity, visible health indicators, habitat conditions, and more), each objective removes one obstacle.

01Watch less

Efficient, coral-aware video understanding

Identify and keep the most informative visual evidence instead of processing every frame or sampling at fixed intervals.

  • Locate coral. Separate coral regions from water, rock, and other objects with SAM 2.
  • Select keyframes by image clarity, viewing angle, coral coverage, and distinct visual features.
  • Allocate attention to coral regions with complex structure or uncertain observations, not to background.
  • Compact visual tokens. A hybrid convolution–attention vision encoder emits few tokens at high resolution, lowering time-to-first-token on long videos.
DeliverableEfficient video-processing prototype, compared with conventional frame selection on frames processed, compute, and observation accuracy.
02Reason

Coral-specific reasoning across video frames

Combine observations from multiple selected frames to build a complete, consistent understanding of each colony and the surrounding habitat.

  • Associate regions across frames so each colony keeps one identity and is counted once.
  • Domain fine-tuning: LoRA instruction tuning on multi-view coral question answering, with coral-specific knowledge in the prompt.
  • Reasoning traces: the model observes, compares views, concludes, and verifies, and each step must cite a frame.
  • Fuse by agreement. When frames conflict or visibility is poor, the system reports uncertainty instead of forcing an answer.
DeliverableMulti-frame coral-understanding framework, evaluated across the 16 dimensions against image-based and video-based VLM baselines.
03Verify

Evidence-grounded analysis and scientific reporting

  • Structured reports organized by the 16 dimensions, summarizing coral characteristics and visible ecological conditions.
  • Frame citations: every observation links to supporting frames and timestamps.
  • Claim verification checks generated statements against the visual evidence; evidence-aware preference tuning discourages unsupported claims.
  • Observed vs. interpreted: directly visible traits are separated from biological interpretations that need expert confirmation; missing evidence is stated as "uncertain."
DeliverableAutomated reporting framework, assessed by HBOI experts for accuracy, completeness, usefulness, and unsupported claims.
04 / Hypotheses

What we expect to show.

H1

Coral-aware frame and region selection with compact visual tokens cuts processing time by at least 30% while keeping coral-understanding accuracy comparable to processing all frames.

H2

Multi-frame reasoning with a coral-tuned VLM is more accurate and more consistent across viewpoints than single-image VLMs, and correctly recognizes when evidence is insufficient.

H3

Evidence-grounded reporting lowers the hallucination rate and gives correct frame references for supported observations, as judged by marine-science experts.

05 / Evaluation

CoralLens-Bench

A reproducible benchmark for how efficiently and reliably VLMs analyze underwater coral video and write scientifically meaningful reports.

CoralVQA

Public coral VQA data: 12,805 images and 277,653 question–answer pairs across 16 dimensions, for coral-specific visual understanding.

Underwater video

Representative coral footage from public sources and FAU Harbor Branch, extending evaluation from single images to videos.

Expert review

Selected frames and segments annotated or reviewed by Dr. Ouyang's HBOI team, who also rate the generated reports.

Questions and how we measure them

Is it efficient?
Percentage of frames retained, visual tokens, processing time and time-to-first-token, and compute cost, while checking that important coral observations are preserved.
Is coral understanding accurate?
Correctness across the 16 dimensions on CoralVQA and expert-reviewed video.
Is it consistent across views?
Agreement of answers about the same colony across frames, lighting, and turbidity.
Does it know when it cannot tell?
How often the system correctly reports insufficient visual evidence.
Is the reasoning faithful?
Whether each reasoning step is supported by the frame it cites.
Are the reports reliable?
Factual accuracy, completeness of supported observations, hallucination rate, correctness of frame references, and expert ratings of usefulness.

08 / Impact

Why it matters, and what comes next.

Intellectual merit

  • Coral-aware efficiency: keyframe and region selection plus compact visual tokens for long underwater videos.
  • Multi-view reasoning: a coral-tuned VLM whose answers are fused across viewpoints with explicit uncertainty.
  • Faithful reasoning and reporting: frame-cited reasoning traces and claim verification for scientific reports.

Broader impacts

Faster, evidence-supported review of reef footage for marine scientists, conservation groups, and resource managers. Targets: at least one open-source CoralLens prototype and shareable CoralLens-Bench components; at least 30% less processing time at comparable accuracy; at least two students trained in AI and marine science; and a feedback session with HBOI researchers. The framework is designed to extend to sea turtles, manatees, fish, and other marine species.

External funding path

  • Primary: NSF Information and Intelligent Systems (IIS), efficient and trustworthy vision-language intelligence for underwater monitoring.
  • Secondary: NOAA programs for coral reef monitoring and marine ecosystem conservation.
  • At least one external proposal by December 2027, targeting about $500,000–$750,000, built on CoralLens-Bench, validated video analysis, and evidence-grounded reporting.
09 / Glossary

Key terms in plain language.

Vision-language model (VLM)
An AI trained on images and text together that can answer questions about what it sees and write descriptions.
Keyframe
A frame kept from a video because it is sharp and adds new information; near-duplicates are dropped.
SAM 2
"Segment Anything 2," an AI that outlines objects in images and follows them through video.
Visual tokens
The small pieces an image is turned into before the language model reads it. Fewer tokens means faster answers.
LoRA fine-tuning
A light way to adapt a large model to a new domain, here coral imagery, by training a small number of extra parameters.
Reasoning trace
The model's step-by-step explanation (chain of thought). In CoralLens each step must point to a frame.
Hallucination
A statement a model makes that the evidence does not support.
CoralVQA
A public dataset of coral images with question–answer pairs covering 16 coral-understanding dimensions.
